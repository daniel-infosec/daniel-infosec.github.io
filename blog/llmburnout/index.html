LLM Fatigue for the AI-Pilled

Are even the most AI-Pilled amongst us dealing with AI burnout? Probably, yeah

November 20, 2026

Daniel Wyleczuk-Stern

6-Minute Read

Twitter

Facebook

Reddit

LinkedIn

Pinterest

Email

Intro

My family has a few mottos. “Do Less” inspired by one of the greatest films ever (despite Russell Brand’s tarnished reputation. I consider the fact that he’s an ass in the movie to just be him playing himself) and “Life sucks, then you die”. I swear we’re actually a cheery lot. So what does this have to do with LLM burnout? Well, it feels like life is a lot more of the latter motto. Despite me writing the least amount of code in a year since high school, it feels like I’m doing more than ever as an IC. And I’m trying to figure out why. I’m going to share, in artisanally crafted words (no AI writing here), why I believe life sucks even for the most AI-pilled amongst us.

Note - my thoughts mainly apply to security engineers and developers at companies that encourage and/or enforce their employees use AI heavily.

Every IC is a Manager

I posted about this on LinkedIn recently, but every IC is now a manager. My job used to be writing code, implementing security features, doing secure designs and reviews, etc. Now, most of my and others’ knowledge has been encoded into AI skills and my job is to review reports and outputs. If they’re not good, I provide coaching and training to improve the capabilities of my “subordinates”. This is pretty different work from what I’ve done for most of my career. I did a year as a manager and went back to being an IC because I enjoyed being hands-on. While tech has always rapidly evolved, it mainly changed “what” we did. This is the largest change in “how” developers work that I can remember in my career.

Decision Overload

As part of the above, once your AI systems are up and running, decision overload takes over. Two years ago, I’d get a ticket for a detection fix. I’d spend an hour or two deciding on what the fix is and then a couple hours implementing. That implementation time used different brain muscles than the decision muscles. For a lot of experienced coders, this is when you’d enter “flow state” and programming could be relaxing. That step is now gone. Instead, LLMs slap you in the face with choice after choice that you have to make. And making these correctly can be exhausting. I’m not even talking about manual mode vs auto mode. Tougher decisions like “how do you want to handle auto scaling” or “should we split this module up into two for better testing speed or would that result in too much duplicate code”. These are tougher architectural choices that require deliberate thought and doing that all day without the reprieve of implementation is tiring. But we’re also still accountable for the overwhelming amount of decisions. Implementation has gotten easier, but that makes every day filled with the hardest stuff.

Skills Becoming Useless

I’ve spent a lot of time working on developing coding and security skills. I took AP computer science in school. I worked (moderately) hard in college. I’ve done some CTFs, reverse engineering challenges, programming challenges, etc. I’ve spent over a decade working and learning in industry. Now it feels like none of that matters. What I’ve spent years getting good at is now captured by a bunch of weighted numbers in a complicated binary blob of data I don’t fully understand. Now I’m filled with this sense of despondency. Does the stuff rattling around in my grey matter make a difference? Or am I just a moderately articulate Carbonoid creature translating LLM output into meat-space?

Being Left Behind

ChatGPT 3.5 came out almost exactly 4 years ago. People were pasting functions asking it for help and it would deliver moderately useful answers. Now we’re being instructed to build software factories where our applications fully repair and build themselves using LLMs. OpenAI and Anthropic have recursive self-improvement on their models. The rate of acceleration is increasing. I can’t imagine taking a 6-month sabbatical now. I’d come back like a time traveler from the 1800s fascinated and inept in the modern world. I personally know the challenge this has placed on folks debating how (or if) to take parental leave. FOMO governs so much of my day-to-day decisions and it’s becoming really exhausting.

We’re All Going to Die

Back in 2024, I almost got a job at Anthropic. I got to the final round when they passed over me to another candidate. (Insert Squidward staring at SpongeBob and Patrick playing meme. I’m definitely not a little bitter…) Anyways. One of the reasons I was enthralled with the company was their heavy focus on safety. I wrote that in my cover letter and enjoyed the interview conversations where that came up. Now it seems like the major AI labs care as much about safety as Elizabeth Holmes cares about defrauding investors. It’s hard not to look at things like Hugging Face, ARTEX, or any of the other numerous issues and warnings and think “Huh… maybe teaching sand to think was a mistake.”

On the other side of the coin, if the machines don’t kill us all, how can we not be worried about our futures? Many leading tech executives are saying “have the LLM replace your rote work so you can work on harder stuff”. At some point though, will there be jobs for humans? For those of us with kids, how do you approach teaching them skills and values that matter when it seems like knowledge work may disappear? It’s hard to carry around this sense of existential dread into your daily work.

Relevant YouTube video: youtube.com/watch?v=7Pq-S557XQU&vl=en

Eat the Rich

While knowledge workers struggle with all this, the top cats are eating well. The wealth divide is growing exponentially, executives profit off of labor more than ever, and they’re bragging about it. If I read another article glazing an actor who got even richer or a CEO in the Epstein files telling their employees to work harder, I’m going to lose it. I’m not doing work I enjoy as much, I have decision fatigue, I’m scared about the future, and the rich are profiting off of my misery.

Conclusion

I think it’s pretty easy to understand why folks are getting burned out. Talking with friends and peers, lots of folks are experiencing one or more of the above. I don’t have good advice. I’m not a therapist. Life sucks, then you die. I hope it gets better though.

(This isn’t a cry for help. I have a support structure. Other people may not. Talk to your friends and family.)
